# N7 development lineage audit (read-only)

Scope: the four already exposed N7 development episodes on tasks 0, 3, 6, and 9. This is a CPU replay of native evidence, not an algorithm experiment or a physical counterfactual. The source reports and every one of the 33 guided-decision bundles were SHA-256 checked. The script scanned 6,459 event-bundle summaries, loaded only the 33 guided bundles, checked the native event counts against summaries, and verified each selected latent against the canonical terminal row, decoder and committed action. Reproduce with `python audit_lineage.py` from this directory. The full decision-level numbers, source hashes and metric definitions are in `lineage-audit-v1.json`.

| Task | Episode outcome | Guided decisions | Archive best > terminal best | Terminal best > selected | Mean archive→terminal quality gap |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 0 | failure | 17 | 16 | 1 | 0.00256 |
| 3 | failure | 10 | 9 | 0 | 0.00735 |

The final QD archive's highest native provider quality exceeded the highest quality in the last canonical 16 in 31/33 decisions. Restricting execution to that last row therefore drops the archive's highest scored latent in most decisions; the exact archive-best fingerprint was executed in only 2/33. By contrast, N7's `terminal_grasp_proximity` selector chose the terminal row's provider-quality best in 32/33 decisions. The sole terminal selection quality gap was 0.00297 at task 0, global step 352. All values compare native `effective_quality` within one decision and stage, where higher is better. They do not measure task-success regret.

The archive→terminal gap occurred in 25/27 guided decisions of failed episodes (mean 0.00433) and 6/6 of successful episodes (mean 0.00693). Thus this four-episode sample does **not** associate the quality gap with failure; if anything, the observed mean gap is larger in successful episodes. The 33 decisions are correlated within only four episodes and cannot be treated as 33 independent success trials.

The exact immediate allocation chain is observable: archive parent fingerprint, cell and reward → same-index canonical offspring fingerprint and reward → terminal occurrence/index → decoded latent → committed action chunk. Across 5,280 allocated parent slots (33 decisions × 10 iterations × 16 slots), no child kept an identical parent fingerprint and 1,009 children (19.1%) improved native quality relative to their allocated parent. This is evidence that the EDS transformation changes every allocated latent and frequently lowers its proxy quality. It does not show that an unchanged parent would execute better. Original archive insertion occurrence IDs were not recorded (`original_archive_occurrences=None`), so duplicate insertions cannot be assigned a unique full genealogy even when fingerprints match previous canonical rows.

Prior controls constrain the next hypothesis. Q0 already selects the final archive global best; N4 changed that one final-selection mode to terminal-population selection, and N4-E1 yielded 1/4 versus same-round Q0 0/4, below its 2/4 gate. N7 retained the terminal-row contract while changing the descriptor. The older H4 global-quality anchor reserved parent slots and recorded 425 allocation rescues, yet its frozen short development comparison remained 0/6 for candidate and control. Directly restoring archive-best execution would substantially resemble Q0, and merely assigning a global-best parent slot repeats H4. The present audit supports no claim that either change would improve physical task success. The more defensible next experiment, if any, must isolate a new parent-to-offspring mechanism and require an online success gate, not a proxy-quality-only gate.

Post-execution evidence in the same four traces: `pre_reset_outcome` and `step_returned` occur for 2,066 executed environment steps. `pre_reset_outcome.observation` contains actual robot EEF position/quaternion and gripper position/velocity, plus reward, terminated and success; `contemporaneous_target_pose` is `None`. `step_returned` records termination/truncation/success. All 33 guided metric rows have `target_distance_before`, but `target_distance_after` is `None`. The subsequent audit below uses the **already policy-visible** target keypoint from the next `decision_context` as a chunk-boundary target-position observation. There is still no step-by-step target pose, contact sensor, grasp state, or unexecuted-action outcome.

## Existing target-keypoint motion at adjacent chunk boundaries

`audit_target_motion.py` SHA-verifies the relevant native bundles and pairs all 33 guided decisions with the next chunk's `decision_context`. The grasp selector's target index is 0 throughout. The current `scoring_inputs.keypoints[0]` agrees with the full-precision `decision_context.keypoints[0]` within 2 mm. `projection_origin.source_eef_position` supplies the already policy-visible EEF start, and the last `pre_reset_outcome.robot0_eef_pos` supplies actual EEF position after the eight executed actions. See `target-motion-audit-v1.json` for all 33 pairs. `KeypointTracker.get_keypoint_positions` is normally object-pose-backed, but its code can fall back to an initial/static point when pose tracking is unavailable. The bundles say `target_observation=existing_chunk_keypoint_read` and do **not** persist the per-keypoint tracking-validity status. A measured keypoint shift is real in the recorded input stream; zero shift alone does not certify a physically stationary object.

| Episode group | Guided pairs | Target keypoint shift ≥1 cm | Mean target shift | Mean actual EEF motion |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Failed tasks 0 and 3 | 27 | 7 | 0.0096 m | 0.0796 m |
| Successful tasks 6 and 9 | 6 | 0 | 0.0004 m | 0.1186 m |

The shifted chunks were task 0 at steps 24, 296, 304, 344 and 512, and task 3 at steps 8 and 16. Their target shifts were 1.2–7.1 cm. Of these seven, four had target/actual-EEF motion cosine ≥0.7, all in task 0; task 3's two were -0.19 and 0.27. The successes may move the object later in *unguided* chunks, which this narrow analysis intentionally does not cover. This is a descriptive four-episode association, not an independent trial count or a diagnosis of failure.

`audit_shift_correlates.py` adds the exact selected action's gripper signs and the selected candidate's minimum **projected** 3D EEF–target distance (`shift-correlates-v1.json`). Four of seven shifted chunks contained at least one +1 gripper command, compared with nine of 26 below-threshold chunks; +1 is the N5 selector's positive/closing-command criterion, not observed grasp. All seven shifted chunks had projected minimum reach ≤6 cm, versus 17/26 below-threshold. In task 0, four of five shifted chunks had +1 commands; both shifted task 3 chunks had eight -1 commands. Thus movement can occur without a closing command, and a close projection does not certify contact. The prior N9 lateral-sweep penalty already changed six decisions yet lost task 9's success, so the observed movement does not justify simply strengthening that penalty. A new online contact or carry hypothesis would need to use existing chunk-boundary feedback with an explicit validity/fallback rule and prove a task-success gain in matched online evaluation; this audit does not establish such a gain.
